# Learning roadmap

Planned milestones, not completed qualifications. Progress should be recorded through actual notes, workflow outputs, corrections, and demonstrations.

## First 30 days

| Period | Focus | Evidence to produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | AI literacy and automotive research terminology | A short glossary in my own words; one full-paper reading note |
| Week 2 | Prompting, source verification, and automation basics | A five-source literature-triage pilot with unedited outputs and correction log |
| Week 3 | Workflow structure and agents | A diagram distinguishing fixed workflow steps from agent decisions; a documented human approval point |
| Week 4 | Research reproducibility | One documented scholarly search and an initial screened evidence table |

## Questions to learn to answer

- What is the difference between an AI model, an assistant, an automated workflow, and an agent?
- What can an abstract establish, and what requires full-text inspection?
- How do fault diagnosis, anomaly detection, and predictive maintenance differ?
- Why can a random row split exaggerate performance when samples come from the same vehicle or trip?
- What does a useful uncertainty statement look like?
- Which findings might transfer to Nigerian settings, and which need new evidence?

## Practical portfolio standard

For each workflow or project, document the problem, inputs, steps, tools actually used, output, verification, limitations, and what I learned. Describe manual prototypes as manual; label future automation as planned. Add certificates only after earning them, and research findings only after completing the underlying work.
